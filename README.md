# Quality-Diversity Evolution of Trading Strategies With an LLM Research Team

![Quality-diversity strategy evolution architecture](docs/project-graphic.png)

Strategy research is bottlenecked by a single researcher's throughput and by the tendency of search to collapse onto one familiar style. This project asks whether a genuinely diverse population of trading strategies can be discovered automatically — and whether preserving that diversity costs anything in quality. It builds an autonomous research loop in which a language model proposes a falsifiable hypothesis, turns it into a complete trading strategy, and evaluates the result against realistic trading costs. Every outcome is distilled into a reusable insight that conditions the next proposal, so search quality compounds across iterations instead of resetting.

The central design question is how quality and distinctiveness are reconciled. Strategies are rewarded not only for how well they trade but for how different they are from what is already known, and the search is structured so that no single style can crowd out the rest. To make the question answerable, two scoring philosophies are run to completion on identical universes and data and compared directly: one that structurally preserves a spread of distinct strategies, and one that jointly optimizes the trade-offs among risk-adjusted return, return magnitude, and drawdown. The comparison is reported honestly along three axes — the effective number of genuinely different styles the search retains, how much the resulting strategies actually trade, and whether the best strategies' out-of-sample results remain statistically credible once corrected for the sheer number of trials the search ran. No strategy is trusted that has not paid real commissions and slippage or that has seen the held-out data before its final evaluation.

The system is exercised on two independent markets — a fifteen-name equity universe and two equity-index futures — each run separately and reported on its own, so conclusions are not an artifact of a single asset class. Implementation details, environment setup, and the staged run sequence are specified in `runbook.md`.
